## Dictation 2 (Oct 30)
- Resident should ask for ABI. Show ABI + PVR: waveforms and numbers.
- ABI non-compressible at the ankle (e.g., 1.7). Toe pressure <30 (e.g., 23) with flattened digit waveforms on the wound foot.
- Resident should state WIfI for the wound (to put in the note); the assistant could help by describing the wound and deciding the components.
- Then decide next steps. Must have done the rest of the exam: femoral and popliteal pulses palpable; pedal pulses not palpable.
- Can get a CTA, but must check creatinine first (Cr 1.2).
- CTA: no significant aortoiliac disease, no fem-pop disease, calcific tibial disease (tibials not well seen).
- Angiogram can come first or after CTA. Not about procedural decisions.
- Endovascular is NOT the first choice.
- Tibial angiogram: three-vessel tibial disease (occluded mid-shin with reconstitution via abundant collaterals, or stenotic).
- Should NOT treat at that point: young patient, vein not yet checked. Ordering vein mapping before the cath lab = great.
- Vein mapping: GSV <2 mm throughout, bilaterally (no adequate saphenous vein).
- Endovascular is appropriate ONLY if vein mapping was done and shows no adequate GSV; otherwise surgery first is more appropriate on available data.
- After revascularization (open or endo, one vessel to the foot opened): endocrinology, diabetes control, lipids checked and controlled; medical management mandatory.
- Resident can ask the assistant for guideline-based medical management. No medical management = rated poorly.
- Bad path: in the first visit, resident sends patient to wound care only -> returns ~2 weeks later with worsening wound now involving bone.
- Outcome: everything right -> at most a toe amputation. Flippant -> progression toward amputation.
- Wants to see it as an algorithm, then decide bad decisions and what's next.

## Dictation 6 (Oct 30): ideal path + failure modes (supersedes earlier branch sketches where they conflict)
IDEAL PATH
- Small toe wound -> exam -> CTA optional -> vein mapping BEFORE angiogram -> angiogram: three-vessel tibial disease -> no single-segment GSV, so endovascular treatment of one of the three vessels (balloon angioplasty), supported by BEST-CLI -> patient does well, wound heals.
- From the first visit: wound cared for appropriately, offloaded, risk factors adjusted. Appropriate referrals, but the resident manages most things.
- Resident should use the AI assistant to find the latest guidelines for BP, lipids, diabetes, etc., and manage them. Should NOT defer to PCP ("per PCP management" is an available but wrong option).
- Smoking: check status, offer cessation; continued smoking -> worse outcomes.
- Antiplatelet therapy indicated; follow AHA guidelines. They don't need to memorize; they can use the assistant.
FAILURE MODES
- Angiogram without prior vein mapping: happens, not a fail. Vein mapping afterward shows inadequate vein -> second angiogram for treatment.
- "You have enough blood to heal, move on": returns at 2 weeks with toe osteomyelitis -> loses the toe, requires revascularization later.
- OR without vein mapping: vein inadequate -> prosthetic or CryoVein (both bad) -> infection -> explant -> below-knee amputation.
- LDL not checked/managed: good wound-healing outcome, but MI.
- Diabetes not checked/managed (no A1c, no endo referral or management): revascularized, more flow, but foot doesn't heal -> (below-)knee amputation.
- No offloading: keeps walking, wound doesn't heal despite revascularization -> amputation.
- Some percentage of amputation patients have a postoperative MI and die.
